Two centuries of Bangla journalism

Bangla-language journalism is older than Bangladesh itself. Samachar Darpan, printed at Serampore from 1818, is counted among the first Bangla newspapers, and the press reached eastern Bengal soon after — Dhaka Prokash began publishing from Dhaka in 1861 and gave the region its own journalistic voice.

The twentieth century turned that voice political. Through the Pakistan period, the Language Movement and the struggle that led to independence in 1971, newspapers were not neutral observers; they were part of how a national identity was argued into existence. Dainik Ittefaq, which grew under editor Tofazzal Hossain Manik Mia, became the most prominent example, and papers like Sangbad shared that role.

The 1990s brought a different kind of change. A new generation of dailies arrived with cleaner layouts, professional editing and a stated commitment to investigative reporting, and readers' expectations of what a newspaper should look like and deliver shifted permanently.

What separates one daily from another

On any given morning the front pages will overlap heavily — the same parliamentary session, the same flood, the same match. The differences show up in everything around the lead story: which sections get space, which columnists appear, how much room goes to business or district news, and which stories a paper chooses to follow for weeks rather than days.

Circulation leaders such as Prothom Alo built their position on broad national coverage and a strong Friday magazine culture. Others carved out specific strengths — detailed economic reporting, agriculture and rural affairs, education and exam coverage, or sport. Reading two papers rather than one is the cheapest way to notice what each leaves out.

Editorial positions differ too, and in a politically polarised media environment that is worth keeping in mind. A directory like this one lists publishers across the spectrum without endorsing any of them; the judgement stays with you.

এর একটা সুবিধা আছে, একটা ঝুঁকিও আছে। সুবিধা হলো গতি; ঝুঁকি হলো তাড়াহুড়োয় ভুল তথ্য ছড়িয়ে পড়া। বড় কোনো ঘটনার প্রথম আধা ঘণ্টার খবর প্রায়ই অসম্পূর্ণ থাকে, পরে সংশোধন আসে। তাই গুরুত্বপূর্ণ খবর শেয়ার করার আগে অন্তত দুটি পত্রিকায় মিলিয়ে নেওয়া ভালো অভ্যাস।

এই তালিকার প্রতিটি লিংক সরাসরি সংশ্লিষ্ট পত্রিকার নিজস্ব ওয়েবসাইটে নিয়ে যায়। আমরা কোনো সংবাদ কপি করি না বা নিজেদের সাইটে ধরে রাখি না — পাঠক যেন মূল উৎসেই পৌঁছান, সেটিই উদ্দেশ্য।

Getting more out of a daily

Read the dateline and the byline. A report filed by the paper's own correspondent from the district carries different weight than an agency copy rewritten in Dhaka, and the dateline tells you which you are looking at.

Separate reporting from opinion. Editorials and op-eds are argument, not news, even when they sit on adjacent pages. Many readers form impressions from columns while believing they are reading reportage.

Follow corrections. Papers that publish corrections are not less reliable than those that never do; usually the opposite is true. A publication willing to say it got something wrong is one worth more of your attention.

FAQ

Which is the largest Bangla daily newspaper?

Prothom Alo is generally regarded as the largest-circulation Bangla daily in Bangladesh, with Dainik Ittefaq, Kaler Kantho, Jugantor, Samakal and Bangladesh Pratidin also among the most widely read. Circulation figures move over time, so treat any ranking as approximate.

Are these newspapers free to read online?

Almost all Bangladeshi dailies publish their web editions free of charge, funded by advertising rather than subscriptions. A few put magazine supplements or archive access behind registration, but day-to-day news is open.
