From the evening bulletin to the endless feed

Satellite television arrived in Bangladesh in the 1990s and private news channels followed, breaking the single-broadcaster era. The nightly bulletin became an appointment that households planned around, and live reporting from the scene changed how fast the country learned about a cyclone, a verdict or an election result.

Streaming dismantled that schedule. Channels such as Somoy, Jamuna, Channel 24, Independent, Ekattor, NTV and ATN News now publish continuously to their own websites and video channels, and a two-minute clip of a single exchange routinely reaches more people than the bulletin it came from.

This has made television newsrooms some of the largest digital publishers in the country. Their sites carry written reports alongside video, which means they compete directly with the newspaper portals rather than occupying a separate medium.

What television does that text cannot

Some stories need to be seen. Flooding, fire, crowd size, the condition of a road or an embankment, the atmosphere in a courtroom corridor — footage conveys these in a way a paragraph cannot, and live reporting from the location is television's enduring strength.

Television also reaches people who do not read comfortably in either Bangla or English, which matters in a country where literacy varies widely by region and age. For many households it remains the primary source of national news.

The limitation is depth. A broadcast item runs for a minute or two and cannot carry the documentation, figures and caveats a written investigation can. The channels that handle this best pair their video with a full written report on the website — worth looking for when a story matters to you.

FAQ

Can I watch Bangladeshi TV news live online?

Yes. Most national channels stream their broadcast free on their own website and official video channel, and many also run a separate live page during major events such as elections or budget sessions.

Do TV channel websites carry written news as well?

Most do. Alongside video, the larger channels publish full written reports, which are easier to read on a slow connection and usually contain more detail than the broadcast item.

Why does the same clip appear on many pages?

Clips are widely re-uploaded by third-party pages, often without the original date or context. Watching from the channel's own site or verified page is the reliable way to see when a clip is from and what surrounded it.
