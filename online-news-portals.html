How online-only news took hold

For most of the country's history, a news organisation meant a printing press. That changed in the 2000s when bdnews24.com launched as Bangladesh's first internet-only news service, built around wire-style speed rather than a daily deadline. It proved that an outlet could be serious, staffed and profitable without ever printing a page.

A wave of portals followed. Jagonews24, Bangla Tribune, Dhaka Post, Risingbd, Banglanews24 and others built newsrooms designed from the start for the web: short lead paragraphs, constant updates, heavy use of photographs and, increasingly, vertical video.

Cheap mobile data was the other half of the story. Once a smartphone and a data pack cost less than a monthly newspaper subscription, the portal became the default way for millions of people to read the news.

What portals do better — and worse

Speed is the obvious advantage. Election results, court verdicts, match scores and weather warnings appear within minutes, and a story can be updated as many times as the facts change rather than frozen at midnight.

Range is the second advantage. Without a fixed page count, a portal can run a short district item that would never survive the competition for space in a printed paper, which is genuinely useful for local news.

The costs are real too. Publishing continuously rewards being first, and being first rewards publishing before verification is complete. Headlines are written for clicks; early reports get numbers wrong; stories from social media are sometimes picked up before anyone confirms them. The better portals correct quickly, but corrections rarely travel as far as the original.

Portals, aggregators and social feeds

It is worth keeping three things apart. A news portal employs reporters and editors and is accountable for what it publishes. An aggregator — including this directory — organises links to those publishers without producing journalism of its own. A social feed shows you whatever an algorithm predicts will hold your attention, from any source or none.

Most misinformation reaches people through the third category while appearing to come from the first. Going directly to a publisher you recognise, which is what the links below are for, removes that layer of ambiguity.

FAQ

Which was the first online news portal in Bangladesh?

bdnews24.com is widely credited as the country's first internet-only news organisation, launching in the mid-2000s and establishing the model that later portals followed.

Are online portals less reliable than printed newspapers?

Not inherently — several portals maintain large professional newsrooms. The difference is the publishing cycle: continuous publishing means early reports are more likely to be incomplete, so it is worth rechecking an important story a few hours later.

Do these sites work well on a slow connection?

It varies. Portals are generally lighter than video-heavy sites, but many load large advertisements. Most offer a mobile app that uses less data than the full website.
